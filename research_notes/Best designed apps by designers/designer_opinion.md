# Best-Designed Apps According to Working Designers and Design Leaders (practitioner opinion, as of Oct 2026)

Research note: about 22 search/fetch calls. Several primary sources could not be fetched: lennyslightninground.com gave a DNS failure on two tries, and Reddit threads did not surface in search. Counts below therefore come from the few tallies I could verify. Treat them as directional. Each opinion is dated where possible.

## Q1. When designers and practitioners are asked "what's the best-designed app / which product do you admire?", which apps do they name?

### Takeaway

No rigorous, designer-only survey exists. The closest quantitative signal is a fan-made tally of Lenny's Podcast "Lightning Round" favorite-product answers. Its guests are mostly product and design leaders. **Arc Browser leads it (8 mentions)**, followed by ChatGPT (7), Granola (6), and Superhuman, Notion and Slack (5 each). Hacker News "best-designed software" threads lean toward pro/power tools (Rhino3D, Ableton, Blender, Bloomberg Terminal) and developer products (Stripe, Linear, Basecamp, Redis/Postgres). They are an engineer-heavy audience rather than a design one.

### Cited Findings

**Lenny's Podcast Lightning Round tally (product/design leaders, roughly 2022–2026)**

- The Lightning Round includes the question "Do you have a favorite app or product you've recently discovered that you really love?" It does not state its date range and is unofficial — [Lenny's Lightning Round (fan site), via search snippet](https://www.lennyslightninground.com/products/). ### Inferences

- Combining the Lenny tally, HN and the design-press material in Q4/Q5 gives a rough ranking of the most-cited "best-designed" products in 2023–2026 practitioner discourse:
  1. **Linear**: the most-referenced as a craft benchmark and trend-setter (Q4).
  2. **Stripe**: website, dashboard and docs; the "make it like Stripe" client request (Q4).
  3. **Arc**: top Lenny favorite (8), now in maintenance mode (Q6).
  4. **Superhuman**: 5 Lenny mentions; speed and keyboard.
  5. **Notion**: 5 Lenny mentions plus HN.
  6. **Apple first-party / iPod**: the long-standing reference for motion and native feel.
  7. **Things 3** and **Flighty**: Apple-platform craft exemplars.
  8. **Figma**: 3 Lenny mentions.
  9. **Raycast**: keyboard-first; also cited as a Linear-style site.
  10. **Granola**: a newer AI-era favorite with 6 Lenny mentions.
- "Favorite product" is not the same as "best-designed". Many Lenny answers such as ChatGPT, Waymo and Eight Sleep reflect novelty or utility, not UI craft.
- The engineer-heavy HN audience values power, density and keyboard control over visual polish. That differs from the visual-craft values of designer audiences such as Dive Club and Config.

## Q2. Which apps do Mobbin, Refero, Page Flows and similar libraries showcase, and which do teams benchmark against?

### Takeaway

I found no public "most-referenced apps" ranking from Mobbin, Refero or Page Flows. Mobbin's own marketing uses Airbnb, Netflix, Snapchat, Tinder and Duolingo as flagship examples. Agency writing says clients ask most often to "make it like Stripe", followed by Intercom and Apple.

### Cited Findings

- Mobbin describes its library as "100,000+ iOS and Android screenshots from 500+ best-designed apps such as Airbnb, Netflix, and Snapchat". A later profile cites 1,000+ apps, 585,000+ screens and 134,000+ flows, and "400,000+ designers and product folks" as users. This is marketing copy — [Exit Five jobs listing](https://jobs.exitfive.com/company/mobbin); [Parse brand profile](https://parse.gl/brands/mobbin-com)
- The Eleken agency (page updated June 2026): "Our clients want their apps and websites to mimic Intercom, Apple, and, most frequently — Stripe." — [Eleken, "Make it like Stripe"](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/making-it-like-stripe)
- Design write-ups group Stripe, Linear and Vercel as the reference set for "premium" SaaS UI. They describe high contrast, a monochrome palette with one accent, and sharp type, and say "Linear is the most consistent execution of this system" — [Pixeldarts](https://www.pixeldarts.com/en/post/four-design-principles-behind-stripe-linear-and-vercel); [Mantlr](https://mantlr.com/blog/stripe-linear-vercel-premium-ui)

### Inferences

- In practice, the benchmark set for SaaS is Stripe, Linear and Vercel. For consumer mobile, pattern libraries keep putting Airbnb, Duolingo, Netflix and Spotify-class apps in front of designers. That shapes what counts as "reference-worthy" partly through exposure, not only through merit.

### Gaps

- No usage data from Mobbin, Refero or Page Flows on most-viewed apps. The libraries do not publish this, or I could not find it.

## Q3. What do NN/g, Baymard and other UX research bodies hold up as exemplary?

### Takeaway

NN/g rarely crowns a single "best app". Where it does name these products, it praises Airbnb's trust mechanics more than its interface, and it uses Stripe's website to teach visual-design principles. I found nothing from NN/g on Duolingo.

### Cited Findings

- NN/g, "Beyond Usability: 3 User Experiences Reshaping Their Industries" (about seven years old, so roughly 2018–2019). It says Airbnb's usability is imperfect but "good enough". It credits the two-way host/guest rating system ("one of the main reasons for which the site is so popular"), real-time availability, and built-in payment — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-user-experiences-reshaping-industries/)
- NN/g, "5 Design Principles in Context: Stripe" (recent video and article), uses the Stripe website to show the five core visual design principles. I saw only the teaser — [NN/g search result](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/10-best-application-uis/) (listing page; I did not retrieve the full Stripe article URL)
- NN/g also runs a long-running "10 Best Application UIs" series, which recognizes enterprise and internal apps rather than famous consumer apps — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/10-best-application-uis/)

### Gaps

- I did not retrieve Baymard's exemplar picks. Its benchmarks are e-commerce sites, mostly behind a paywall.
- I did not get the full text of the NN/g Stripe piece.

## Q4. Which apps have spawned a named design trend or "make it feel like X" shorthand?

### Takeaway

**Linear** is the clearest case. "Linear-style design" and "the Linear effect" are now named aesthetics: dark UI, blurred gradients, glowing micro-motion, dense keyboard-first layouts. **Stripe** is the main "make it like X" client request, for gradients, explanatory animation and docs. Apple's native feel remains the baseline reference.

### Cited Findings

- UX Collective/Bootcamp, "The rise of Linear style design". It describes a dark background, gradient colours, blurs and micro-motion, credits the look to Linear's design under Karri Saarinen, and lists Raycast, Reflect, Twingate and SAASUI as followers — [Bootcamp/UX Collective](https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/the-rise-of-linear-style-design-origins-trends-and-techniques-4fd96aab7646)
- Designer Daryl Ginn called it "the Linear effect": dark backgrounds, blurred gradient art and glowing animated accents across new product pages. AI site generators reinforce the look by defaulting to it — [BuildMVPFast, 2026](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026) (from search snippet; the fetch failed)
- LogRocket (2025) reports that Linear's own site has moved from blue-tinted monochrome to black and white with a few bold accents. Its Feb 2026 piece warns that minimal Linear-style design can lose usability when contrast and affordances weaken — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-do-you-implement-accessible-linear-design-across-light-and-dark-modes/)
- Linear's own redesign write-up: "I often relied on Apple standards, which also helped get close to the feeling of a native app." Apple is the reference behind the reference — [Linear blog](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)
- Mantlr: "Linear's specific grays don't make your interface feel like Linear. The architecture of how Linear uses grays might." — [Mantlr](https://mantlr.com/blog/stripe-linear-vercel-premium-ui)
- Eleken calls imitating Stripe "the sincerest form of failure". Users come to get jobs done, not to admire gradients — [Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/making-it-like-stripe)

### Inferences

- Named trends attach to products whose marketing sites are as distinctive as their apps (Linear, Stripe). Craft-praised consumer apps such as Things and Flighty earn admiration but have not produced a widely copied "style".
- "Duolingo-style gamification" also works as shorthand, given the many asset kits and guides with that title — [vp0 blog](https://vp0.com/blogs/duolingo-style-gamification-ui-assets).

### Gaps

- I found no source that formally documents "Arc-style", "Raycast-style" or "Things-style" as a named trend.

## Q5. Specific qualities designers praise in each frequently cited app

### Takeaway

The praised qualities group into four families. **Craft and quality as culture** (Linear, Stripe, Figma). **Speed and keyboard-first** (Superhuman, Raycast, Linear). **Restraint and refinement over time** (Things 3). **Platform-native, glanceable information design** (Flighty, Apple).

### Cited Findings

**Linear (2024–2026)**

- Karri Saarinen, Linear co-founder and CEO, at Stripe Sessions in May 2024: "I think the craft is more the mindset and the quality or beauty is the output." "If people are not raving about your product, then you probably don't have a good product." "Product quality is the ultimate moat for a business." — [Figma Blog, May 9 2024](https://www.figma.com/blog/stripe-sessions-linear-figma/)
- Saarinen, in a Sequoia profile: if he were building a house, he would not want his tools to be fun; he would want them to be good. The founders tie their taste to Finnish values of simplicity, function and durability — [Sequoia, Linear spotlight](https://sequoiacap.com/article/linear-spotlight/)
- A mobile UX roundup calls Linear one of the cleanest interfaces, with immediate state changes and tight transitions, and says its snappy feedback builds trust. This is agency content — [Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/mobile-ux-design-examples)

**Stripe**

- Katie Dill, Head of Design at Stripe (May 2024): "The quality and details _become_ the differentiation." "Every touchpoint requires that same commitment to craft and beauty." — [Figma Blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/stripe-sessions-linear-figma/)
- Qualities praised by Eleken:
  - clarity and inspiration for a "dull" product
  - animation that explains the product
  - restraint with "no irritating frills"
  - personalized code snippets in the docs
  - tiny details such as randomized typing delays in the payment-form animation

  Source: [Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/making-it-like-stripe)

- On HN, Stripe's dashboard and docs come up repeatedly — [HN 17989798](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17989798)

**Figma**

- Yuhki Yamashita, Figma CPO: "[I]f you need an OKR to convince someone to care about quality, you probably have the wrong team… craft is a culture thing. It's about deciding to care." He also recalls Dylan Field noticing "the frame rate dropped on that interaction—it's not 60 frames per second anymore." — [Figma Blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/stripe-sessions-linear-figma/)

**Flighty (Apple Design Award 2023, Interaction category)**

- Founder Ryan Jones, a former Apple employee, wants the app to feel "almost boringly obvious". It borrows airport departure-board conventions. Commentators praise its Live Activities and Dynamic Island, and Apple featured it in the iPhone 14 keynote. Most of this is the company's own framing plus press — [Apple ADA 2023](https://apple.co/2CTHmHk); [Cirium interview](https://www.cirium.com/?p=52137); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flighty)

**Things 3**

- A 2026 review calls it the "best-designed task manager". It says Cultured Code "have not meaningfully changed the interface since launch — they have tuned it", which is refinement over redesign. Downsides are that it is Apple-only and the price. This is a review site, not a designer quote — [Sunrise Digest](https://thesunrisedigest.com/focus/things-3-review-2026/)

**Superhuman and Raycast**

- 2026 roundups praise Superhuman for treating speed as a design feature, with a keyboard shortcut for nearly every action. They praise Raycast for a single command bar built on a consistent "search, pick, act" model. These are SEO roundups of low to medium reliability — [Reclaim](https://reclaim.ai/blog/productivity-apps); [Efficient.app](https://efficient.app/apps/raycast)

**Arc**

- Lenny's go-to browser and the top Lightning Round pick (see Q1). I could not retrieve the specific design reasons guests gave.

**Airbnb (2025 relaunch)**

- Federico Viticci (MacStories) said the redesigned app is "playful, welcoming and easy to use". He disclosed that Airbnb paid for his trip to LA — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/linked/inside-airbnbs-app-redesign-an-appstories-interview-with-marketing-and-design-leads/). Design lead Teo Connor on the redesign: "it was a bit nuts" — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/it-was-a-bit-nuts-teo-connor-on-designing-the-new-airbnb-app/); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525)

### Inferences

- The qualities named most often across sources are, in order:
  1. care for detail and polish, which design leaders frame as culture
  2. speed and perceived performance, including frame rate and instant state changes
  3. keyboard-first control
  4. restraint and consistency
  5. platform-native integration
- Accessibility is almost never cited as a reason a product is "best-designed". It shows up mainly as a criticism of Linear-style minimalism.

### Gaps

- I found no first-person designer quotes for Things 3, Raycast, Superhuman, Monzo, Revolut, Headspace, Cash App or Spotify. Their praise in these results comes from review or roundup sites.
- I found no data on Apple first-party apps beyond Linear's reliance on Apple standards and the HN iPod comments.

## Q6. Dissenting views and criticisms

### Takeaway

Three reputations have become contested since 2023:

- **Arc**: beloved, but discontinued in favour of the AI browser Dia, with a backlash from fans.
- **Duolingo**: admired for engagement but widely criticised for guilt and streak-based dark patterns.
- **Linear-style**: the look has become a cliché, with accessibility concerns.

Airbnb's May 2025 relaunch got favorable design press, but critics questioned the "services" strategy.

### Cited Findings

- **Arc.** The Browser Company stopped feature development on Arc in 2025 (maintenance only, not open-sourced) and moved to Dia. Josh Miller said they should have stopped Arc "a year earlier", citing a "novelty tax" that made it too different to adopt widely — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1002791); [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/?p=321435)
- TidBITS (June 2025) faulted the company for never acknowledging "the anger and distrust" the announcement caused. Another commentator wrote that the Arc community "feels abandoned and betrayed" — [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/06/20/dia-browser-debuts-with-contextual-ai-chat-but-arc-users-feel-left-behind/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sidtalesara/arc-browser-is-dead-and-dia-isnt-the-answer-a-deeply-frustrated-honest-review-21e8)
- **Duolingo.** Critics cite guilt-driven owl notifications, the streak becoming the goal in place of learning, and loss aversion — [Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/the-art-of-duolingo-notifications-the-subtle-manipulation-of-language-learners/); [Design Buddy](https://designbuddy.substack.com/p/is-duolingo-unethical); [vc.ru 2026](https://vc.ru/design/3017207-onboarding-duolingo-analiz-ux-i-temnyh-patternov)
- An academic paper at the Brazilian HCI conference (IHC), "Teaching or Manipulating? On the Adoption of Bright and Deceptive Patterns by Duolingo", examines both bright and deceptive patterns. I saw only its reference list, not its findings — [SBC IHC](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/ihc/article/view/37677)
- **Airbnb 2025.** Fortune (May 14, 2025) reported the new "services" app being "shot down by critics", and the Octet piece calls services Chesky's most controversial move. I could not retrieve the details of the criticism, which seems to be about strategy and trust rather than the visuals — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/05/14/airbnb-new-app-launch-services-experiences-critics-brian-chesky); [Octet](https://octet.design/journal/airbnb-ceo-brian-chesky-build-everything-app)
- **Linear-style sameness.** AI generators spread the look until it is everywhere, and minimal low-contrast styling risks usability — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-do-you-implement-accessible-linear-design-across-light-and-dark-modes/)
- **Older reputations.** Apollo, the gesture-rich third-party Reddit client, was widely loved but shut down in June 2023 after Reddit's API changes — [Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_(app)>). Basecamp, Pivotal Tracker and Instapaper were HN favorites in 2018 and appear less in recent discourse — [HN 17989798](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17989798)

### Inferences

- Arc's "novelty tax" admission is a useful dissent within design itself. Products designers love for being novel can fail to cross into mainstream use. "Best-designed according to designers" is not "best UX for most people".
- Duolingo is the clearest split. Product and growth practitioners hold it up as a model of engagement, while ethics-minded designers hold it up as a model of manipulation.

### Gaps

- I found no designer-community reaction (Dive Club, Designer News) to Dia's design specifically.
- I did not retrieve the specifics of the Fortune critics on Airbnb.
- I did not retrieve Duolingo's own published streak research as a counterpoint.
