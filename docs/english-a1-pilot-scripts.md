## en-a1-u1-meet: Hello, I’m Alex

Unit 1. Meet the language. Version: `r4jh9z`. Estimated 5 minutes.

Outcome: Say hello and understand a name.

### Step 1: scene

**Meet Ella and Alex**

Tap play. Listen. Tap again to listen again. Ella and Alex meet at a class.

- **Ella:** Hello. I’m Ella. (Ella says hello and gives her name.)
- **Alex:** Hi. I’m Alex. (Alex says hello and gives a name.)

Ella uses British English. Alex uses American English. Both say I’m + name.

### Step 2: teach

**Hello and a name**

| Target    | Meaning support   | Note                                      |
| --------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Hello.    | Say hello.        | Use it when you meet someone.             |
| Hi.       | A friendly hello. | Hi and Hello both work here.              |
| I’m Alex. | My name is Alex.  | I’m means I am. Change Alex to your name. |

### Step 3: choose

Listen. Choose the name.

Listen first: Hi. I’m Alex.

- Ella
- Alex **(key)**
- Ben

Feedback and answer rationale: You heard “I’m Alex.” The name is Alex. Listen again if you need to.

### Step 4: choose

You meet Ella. Say hello.

- Hello. **(key)**
- Alex.
- Ella.

Feedback and answer rationale: Hello greets someone. Alex and Ella are names. Hi also works as a greeting.

### Step 5: build

Use the tiles to say the name Alex. Start with I’m.

Model tile order: I’m Alex.

Extra tiles: None

Feedback and answer rationale: I’m Alex. I’m + name tells someone your name.

### Step 6: speak

Say hello. Say your name. You can use Alex or a made-up name.

Unscored self-practice. Models are hidden until requested.

- Model: Hi. I’m Alex.

Tip: Make the name clear. Say “I’m Alex” together, with a small pause after Hello.

- Self-check: Did I say hello?
- Self-check: Could someone hear the name?

Changed situation: Meet a new person. Say Hello instead of Hi, then give a different name.

## en-a1-u1-listen: Please say that again

Unit 1. Hear and respond. Version: `1n7sa8v`. Estimated 6 minutes.

Outcome: Ask for repetition, understand the reply and say goodbye.

### Step 1: teach

**When you miss something**

| Target                 | Meaning support    | Note                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Please say that again. | Repeat it, please. | Use it when you do not hear or understand. |
| Thanks.                | Thank you.         | Use it when someone helps you.             |
| Bye.                   | Goodbye.           | Use it when you leave.                     |

### Step 2: scene

**Try again**

Ella misses the name. Alex repeats it.

- **Alex:** Hi. I’m Alex. (Alex gives a name.)
- **Ella:** Please say that again. (Ella asks to hear it again.)
- **Alex:** Alex. (Alex repeats the name.)
- **Ella:** Thanks, Alex. I’m Ella. (Ella thanks Alex and gives her name.)

Asking again helps you communicate. You do not need to guess.

### Step 3: choose

You did not hear the name. What helps?

- Bye.
- Please say that again. **(key)**
- Nice to meet you.

Feedback and answer rationale: Please say that again asks for repetition. You can use it as often as you need.

### Step 4: choose

Listen to the repeated name.

Listen first: Alex. I’m Alex.

- Ben
- Ella
- Alex **(key)**

Feedback and answer rationale: The repeated name is Alex.

### Step 5: choose

Listen. Is the person arriving or leaving?

Listen first: Thanks. Bye.

- Leaving **(key)**
- Arriving
- Asking a name

Feedback and answer rationale: Bye closes the conversation. Thanks adds a thank-you.

### Step 6: type

Ask someone to repeat: Please say that ___.

Accepted bounded replies: again / once more / one more time

Hint: Again means one more time.

Feedback and answer rationale: Please say that again. Once more and one more time also ask for a repeat. Use the complete request when you miss something.

### Step 7: speak

You miss a name. Ask to hear it again. Imagine the reply “Ella.” Thank Ella, then say goodbye.

Unscored self-practice. Models are hidden until requested.

- Model: Please say that again.
- Model: Thanks, Ella. Bye.

- Self-check: Did I ask again?
- Self-check: Did I say thanks and goodbye?

Changed situation: This time the repeated name is Alex. Thank Alex and say goodbye.

Model tile order: Thanks, Ella. Bye.

Extra tiles: None

Feedback and answer rationale: Thanks, Ella. Bye. You can thank someone by name before leaving.

### Step 6: rule

## en-a1-u2-meet: From one place, living in another

Unit 2. Meet the language. Version: `11tg0hf`. Estimated 6 minutes.

Outcome: Understand the difference between where someone is from and where they live.

### Step 1: choose

Meet Alex again. Alex asks: What’s your name? Answer as Ella.

- I’m Ella. **(key)**
- Bye, Alex.
- Thanks.

Feedback and answer rationale: I’m Ella gives your name. This uses the introduction from Unit 1.

### Step 2: teach

**Two places**

| Target              | Meaning support              | Note                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Where are you from? | What place do you come from? | You can answer with a city or a country.                              |
| I’m from London.    | London is where I come from. | London is a city. Replace London with your place.                     |
| Where do you live?  | Where is your home now?      | This asks about your home now, not your full address.                 |
| I live in Boston.   | Boston is my home now.       | Boston is a city. Your home can be different from where you are from. |

### Step 3: scene

**Ella’s two places**

Ella is from London. Her home now is Boston.

- **Alex:** Where are you from? (Alex asks where Ella comes from.)
- **Ella:** I’m from London. (Ella comes from London.)
- **Alex:** Where do you live? (Alex asks about Ella’s home now.)
- **Ella:** I live in Boston. (Ella’s home now is Boston.)

### Step 4: choose

Read. Where is Ella’s home now?

Supplied text: I’m Ella. I’m from London. I live in Boston.

- London
- Boston **(key)**
- The text does not say

Feedback and answer rationale: I live in Boston tells you her home now. I’m from London tells you where she comes from.

### Step 5: choose

You want to know where Alex comes from. Ask the question.

- Where do you live?
- What’s your name?
- Where are you from? **(key)**

Feedback and answer rationale: Where are you from? asks where someone comes from. Where do you live? asks about their home now.

### Step 6: match

Match each question to its answer.

- What’s your name? → I’m Ella.
- Where are you from? → I’m from London.
- Where do you live? → I live in Boston.

### Step 7: speak

Be Ella. Say your name, where you are from and where you live.

Unscored self-practice. Models are hidden until requested.

- Model: I’m Ella. I’m from London. I live in Boston.

Tip: Pause between the short sentences. Make each place name clear.

## en-a1-u3-build: Yes, please. No, thank you.

Unit 3. Build a reply. Version: `1bqqejf`. Estimated 6 minutes.

Outcome: Accept a drink, decline it and correct the wrong drink.

### Step 1: teach

**Choose and correct**

| Target           | Meaning support         | Note                                                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Yes, please.     | Yes, I want that.       | Accept an offer.                                       |
| No, thank you.   | No, I do not want that. | Decline an offer politely. No, thanks is also natural. |
| No, tea, please. | Tea is the right drink. | Correct someone who thinks you asked for coffee.       |

### Step 2: scene

**A small mix-up**

Ella wants tea. Ben checks the wrong drink. Both use British English.

- **Ella:** I’d like a tea, please. (Ella orders tea.)
- **Ben:** Coffee? (Ben checks whether she wants coffee.)
- **Ella:** No, tea, please. (Ella corrects the drink.)
- **Ben:** Tea? (Ben checks the corrected drink.)
- **Ella:** Yes, please. (Ella confirms tea.)

### Step 3: choose

You want tea. The server says: Coffee? Choose the correction.

- Yes, please.
- No, tea, please. **(key)**
- No, thank you.

Feedback and answer rationale: No, tea, please corrects the drink and tells the server what you want. No, thank you declines but does not name the right drink.

### Step 4: choose

You do not want water. Someone offers: Water? Choose the reply.

- Yes, please.
- I’d like some water, please.
- No, thank you. **(key)**

Feedback and answer rationale: No, thank you declines the water. The other replies ask for or accept water.

### Step 5: type

Complete the reply: Yes, ___.

Accepted bounded replies: please / thanks / thank you

Hint: Please works here. Thanks or thank you can also accept an offer.

Feedback and answer rationale: Yes, please accepts an offer. Yes, thanks and Yes, thank you can also accept an offer politely. No, thank you would decline it.

### Step 6: build

Ask for water using I’d like. Start with I’d.

Model tile order: I’d like some water, please.

Extra tiles: from

Feedback and answer rationale: I’d like some water, please. Use the whole pattern, changing the drink.

### Step 7: speak

You want coffee. The server checks: Tea? Correct the drink. Then the server says: Coffee? Confirm it.

Unscored self-practice. Models are hidden until requested.

- Model: No, coffee, please.
- Model: Yes, please.

- Self-check: Did I name the drink I wanted?
- Self-check: Did I confirm the correct drink?

Changed situation: Now you want tea and the server says Coffee? Change your correction.

## en-a1-u3-listen: Here or to go?

Unit 3. Hear and respond. Version: `fp2n35`. Estimated 7 minutes.

Outcome: Understand the choice between staying and taking a drink away.

### Step 1: teach

**Stay or leave with your drink**

| Target                | Meaning support              | Note                                                   |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| For here, please.     | I will drink it in the cafe. | A natural reply to a service question.                 |
| To go, please.        | I will take it with me.      | Common in American English.                            |
| To take away, please. | I will take it with me.      | Common in British English. It means the same as To go. |

### Step 2: rule

**Two ways to ask**

For here or to go? asks: stay in the cafe, or take your drink with you? To have here or take away? asks the same thing. Learn to understand both. Use the reply that feels comfortable.

- For here or to go?
- To have here or take away?

### Step 3: scene

**The American model**

Casey asks where Alex wants the drink.

- **Casey:** For here or to go? (Stay in the cafe, or take it with you?)
- **Alex:** To go, please. (Alex will take the drink away.)

### Step 4: scene

**The British model**

Ben asks Ella the same choice.

- **Ben:** To have here or take away? (Stay in the cafe, or take it with you?)
- **Ella:** For here, please. (Ella will drink it in the cafe.)

### Step 5: choose

Listen. Where will the person drink?

Listen first: For here, please.

- In the cafe **(key)**
- Away from the cafe
- The reply does not say

Feedback and answer rationale: For here means staying in the cafe.

### Step 6: choose

Listen. What does the person want?

Listen first: To take away, please.

- To stay in the cafe
- To take the drink with them **(key)**
- To change the drink

Feedback and answer rationale: To take away and to go both mean taking the drink with you.

### Step 7: type

Type either model reply: To go, please. OR To take away, please.

Accepted bounded replies: To go, please. / To take away, please.

Hint: Choose one complete model reply. Both mean taking the drink with you.

Feedback and answer rationale: To take away, please. To go, please. Both choices work for taking your drink with you.

### Step 8: speak

The server asks: For here or to go? Answer. Then imagine the question is To have here or take away? Give the same choice.

Unscored self-practice. Models are hidden until requested.

- Model: To go, please.
- Model: To take away, please.

- Self-check: Did I choose staying or taking the drink away?
- Self-check: Did I understand both questions?

Changed situation: Change your choice: stay in the cafe. Say For here, please.
