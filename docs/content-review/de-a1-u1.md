## Coverage sheet

- **Can do:** greet and leave people, give a name, ask another person's name with the appropriate `du` or `Sie`, introduce somebody, and ask for repetition or slower speech. This serves the simple personal-information exchange in Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1, Sprechen Teil 1. It covers the name portion, not the complete introduction, spelling task, or A1 level. This is the intended repaired unit, based on the German coverage register in `docs/german-curriculum-blueprint.md` and the existing Unit 20 pattern.
- **New items for active use:** 35 targets across five sessions, with no more than eight per session. Stage 2 keeps regional greetings and rarer conversational alternatives recognition-only, superseding Stage 1's proposal to retain all 34 as active targets. Panel round 1 separates the wellbeing answer from its return question, separates the form words and moves the daytime greeting into the short session after Words. Panel round 3 replaces the verb paradigms with whole naming phrases; the total now explicitly includes the two self-introduction phrases modelled in rules. Counts below are individual taught targets, not bundled alternatives.
  - Words, `de-a1-u1-hallo` (8): `Hallo!` (everyday greeting); `Guten Morgen!` (the morning kitchen scene); `Tschüss!` (everyday departure); `Bis später!` (leaving someone the learner will see again); `Wie geht’s?` (the flatmates' opening question); `Gut, danke.` (answering that question); `Und dir?` (returning it); `Es geht.` (a common alternative when not feeling good).

Each target is its own teach entry. Their published review cards stay in Words; the new session reviews the practised departures `Auf Wiedersehen, Frau Wagner!` and `Tschüss, bis später!` without duplicating those original cards.
  - Grammar, `de-a1-u1-ich-bin` (8): `Ich bin …` and `Ich heiße …` (two self-introduction phrases, explicitly modelled as whole phrases in the rules); `Wie heißt du?` (asking a peer's name); `Wie heißen Sie?` (asking formally); `Mein Name ist …` (giving a name at reception or in the exam); `Wer ist das?` (asking who somebody is); `Das ist Maria.` (introducing somebody); `Freut mich!` (a common introduction response). Six targets have teach entries; the two self-introduction phrases are not uncounted verb paradigms. `Bist du Maria?` and `Bist du Jonas?` remain recognition-only examples, not required production.
  - Real talk, `de-a1-u1-du-sie` (8): `Wie bitte?` (polite repetition); `Noch mal, bitte.` (asking to hear something again); `Langsamer, bitte.` (controlling speech speed); `Ich verstehe das nicht.` (stating the difficulty); `Entschuldigung!` (getting attention or apologising); `Ach so!` (showing new understanding); `Genau.` (confirming); `Nee.` (a labelled casual negative response). `Hä?` is recognition-only in the scene note, not a marked lesson answer; its published review card is retained.
  - Exam task, `de-a1-u1-exam` (6): `der Vorname`, `der Familienname`, `der Nachname` (identifying the two name fields and their common labels); `Herr`, `Frau` (separate titles for addressing the person named on the form); `das Formular` (identifying the document).
