# Review record: es-a1-u7 · At the market

A new unit, written through `docs/content-pipeline.md` from the Unit 7 brief in `docs/spanish-a1-units-plan.md`. It replaces the starter lesson `es-market` (retired with `replacedBy: 'es-a1-u7-words'`, as `es-cafe` was for Unit 6); the starter's exact phrases `¿Me da un kilo de jitomates?`, `Medio kilo, por favor.` and `Nada más, gracias.` are taught again word for word so their review intervals carry over (`¿Algo más?` and `¿Cuánto es?` are already Unit 6's).

## Coverage sheet

- **Can do:** ask for quantities and prices at a market stall, understand what the seller says the purchase comes to, check the amount and pay. Exam descriptor: DELE A1 reading, matching short notices and messages to statements (Prueba 1, Tarea 2, R2) and listening, matching short messages to pictures (Prueba 2, Tarea 2, L2); CEFR A1 “can ask people for things and give people things; can handle numbers, quantities, cost and time”. Coverage register rows G10, F07, N03, N04, N16, P02.
- **New items for active use:** 25 in all, 8 at most a session.
  - Words (8): `¿Me da un kilo de jitomates?` (the starter's phrase; `¿Me da …?` is how people ask at a Mexican stall, and a kilo is the unit every price there is given in), `Medio kilo, por favor.` (the starter's phrase; the commonest smaller amount), `el jitomate` (taught on its own before the starter's phrase that uses it), `el aguacate`, `el plátano`, `el queso` (staples on every Mexican market list; nouns with their article, as in Unit 6), `un litro de leche` (the liquid measure, and `leche` is Unit 6), `ciento veinte` (the 101–199 pattern and `cien` versus `ciento`: market totals run past one hundred pesos, and the brief wants hundreds built, not listed).
  - Grammar (8): `¿Cuánto cuesta el queso?`, `¿Cuánto cuestan los aguacates?` (the price question, singular and plural; the DELE listening tasks turn on prices), `¿Cuántos? / ¿Cuántas?` (the question word agreeing with what is counted: the seller's first question and the brief's grammar point), `la manzana`, `los huevos` (to practise `cuántas`/`cuántos` and `de` after a quantity: `un kilo de manzanas`, `una docena de huevos` is recognition), `Es mucho.`, `Un poco más, por favor.`, `Un poco menos, por favor.` (`mucho`/`poco` as the chunks people actually use while the seller weighs).
  - Real talk (8): `¿Cuánto, perdón?` (the natural way to ask for a price again; `¿Puede repetir, por favor?` from Unit 1 is the careful one), `¿Es por kilo?` (confirm the unit before you pay: the brief's “confirm units”), `Nada más, gracias.` (the starter's phrase; the market answer to `¿Algo más?`), `Ahorita no, gracias.` (the everyday Mexican polite no to a seller's offer; labelled Mexican and informal, `No, gracias.` the neutral form), `Mejor medio kilo.` (the pattern `Mejor …` to change one item on the list), `doscientos`, `quinientos` (the hundreds pattern and the one irregular hundred people meet daily: the five-hundred-peso note), `¿Tiene cambio?` (paying with a large note at a stall without a card reader).
  - Exam task (1): `mil` (prices of a thousand and more and street numbers in addresses, which the brief names; `mil` never takes `un`).
- **Recognition only:** `¿Qué le damos?`, `¿Qué va a llevar?` (sellers' openings), `joven`, `güera` (how Mexican sellers address customers; friendly), `¿Le doy una bolsa?` and `una bolsa` (the learner only answers it, with `Sí, por favor.` or `Ahorita no, gracias.`), `la pieza` and `a … la pieza` / `a … el kilo` (priced each or by the kilo), `un poquito`, `muchas`, `pocos` (agreement of `mucho`/`poco` with a noun), `una docena de huevos`, `el tomate` (Spain and much of Latin America for the red tomato; in Mexico it often means the green tomatillo), `la banana` (Argentina and elsewhere for `plátano`), `¿Me pone …?` (Spain's way to ask at a counter), `tianguis` (open-air street market in Mexico), `Aquí tiene.`, `Su cambio.`, the hundreds `trescientos` to `novecientos` in a reference table (`setecientos`, `novecientos` marked as irregular), `centavos`, price labels written `MXN` and `$` with the word `pesos` beside them, and shop signs for the exam task: `Frutas y verduras`, `Quesos`, `Panadería`, `Carnicería`, `Precio por kilo`, `Oferta`.
- **Grammar point:** quantity and price questions that agree with what is counted: `¿Cuánto cuesta?` for one thing, `¿Cuánto cuestan?` for several, `¿Cuántos aguacates? / ¿Cuántas manzanas?`; a quantity joined to its noun with `de` and no article (`un kilo de jitomates`, `medio kilo de queso`, `un litro de leche`); `mucho`/`poco` as fixed chunks (`Es mucho.`, `un poco más`), agreement with a noun shown for recognition only.
- **Sound and spelling:** hearing and reading prices. Number contrasts that are easy to confuse at speed, with their stress: `quince` / `quinientos`, `seis` / `sesenta` / `seiscientos`, `siete` / `setenta` / `setecientos`, `cien` / `ciento`; the written accent on `cuánto`, `cuántos` in a question. Price labels always name their currency (`MXN` or `pesos`), because `$` alone also means dollars; Mexico writes `25.50` with a point, Spain `25,50` with a comma, and both are said `veinticinco cincuenta`. No price in the unit is given as a bare `$`.
- **Brought back:** each session meets at least four earlier taught items, measured by `content:check`.
  - Words: Unit 1 `Buenos días.`, `Por favor.`, `Gracias.`; Unit 3 `uno`, `dos`, `cinco`; Unit 6 `¿Algo más?`, `¿Cuánto es?`, and `¿Me das un café, por favor?` with its usted form `¿Me da…?`.
  - Grammar: Unit 3 `¿Cuántos años tienes?` and Unit 5 `¿Cuántos hermanos tienes?` (the `cuántos` the learner already uses), Unit 3 `dos`, `tres`; Unit 6 `Quiero un té.` pattern (`Quiero …`), this unit's Words items (`el aguacate`, `el queso`, `Medio kilo, por favor.`).
  - Real talk: Unit 1 `¿Puede repetir, por favor?`, `Más despacio, por favor.`; Unit 3 `¿Así está bien?`; Unit 6 `¿Algo más?`, `¿Cuánto es?`, `¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?`; this unit's Words and Grammar items.
  - Exam task: Unit 3 tens and digits (`cinco`, `ocho`), Unit 6 `¿Cuánto es?`, this unit's foods, quantities and hundreds.
- **Scene:** a neighbourhood market in Mexico City on a Saturday morning, buying food for the language exchange's snack table. Cast: Ana and Omar (as before), `Vendedor` at the fruit and vegetable stall (a voice already exists in `src/features/audio/voices.ts`), and `Vendedora` at the cheese and dairy stall (new speaker: a voice to add in stage 2). The learner's own lines: ask for a kilo of tomatoes and half a kilo of cheese, and answer `¿Algo más?` (Words, speak); ask what the avocados cost and say how many with a quantity, then ask for a little more or less (Grammar, speak); ask the price again when it is said fast, check it is per kilo, change one item with `Mejor …`, turn down an offer and pay with a five-hundred-peso note (Real talk, speak); a changed situation for the last speaking step: a different stall, an item priced by the piece rather than the kilo and a budget to keep to, so the learner must ask and decide, not repeat a line (Exam task, speak). The unit call (`es-a1-u7` in `supabase/functions/_shared/unit-calls.ts`, `docs/unit-calls.md`) puts the learner at the `Vendedor`'s stall with a short list and a budget in pesos; the twist is a price given per piece when the learner assumed per kilo, or a total that is wrong, so they have to check it.
- **Exam-format task:** DELE A1 Prueba 1, Tarea 2 format (R2): short notices and price signs from the market matched to statements about what a shopper needs, from a shared pool with extra alternatives that are not used; and Prueba 2, Tarea 2 format (L2): short heard messages (`Dos kilos de manzanas, por favor.`, `Doce huevos.`) matched to signs or written purchases from a pool with unused options, where the decoys separate number from unit (`dos kilos` / `doce`, `medio kilo` / `un kilo`, `por pieza` / `por kilo`). The app has no picture options yet, so the L2 pool uses written signs and labels, never English text that gives the answer away. Then a total heard and typed, and a short written shopping list with quantities. Practice towards the task, not a score.

## Writing notes

- `mil` is taught in the Exam task's rule table (`A thousand, and prices on labels`), not as a review card, because an Exam task adds no cards (`tests/spanish-units.test.ts`); the unit has 24 cards. The starter lesson `es-market` is retired to `es-a1-u7-words`.
