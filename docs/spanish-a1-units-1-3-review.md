## Decisions that need native and teacher scrutiny

1. **Reference variety and register.** Mexican Spanish remains the consistent speaking reference. Check natural greetings, questions, polite repair, nationality forms and contextual tú/usted. Do not judge register solely by age. Diego's Spanish origin does not make his synthetic voice evidence of a Spain accent; no Spain-accent comprehension claim is made by this pilot. Broader regional input remains planned.
2. **Naturalness in context.** Check every translation, distractor and accepted response, including `Soy estudiante`, `Hablo español e inglés`, `¿Dónde vive?`, third-person introductions and the surname/phone correction at the desk. Flag instructions that blur speaking as a character and introducing them to someone else.
3. **Numbers and spelling.** Verify each actual audio digit and letter against its transcript. Pay particular attention to 096, 7041, every ten-digit number, 15 versus 50, ñ, hache, letter-name variants and stress in dieciséis, veintidós, veintitrés and veintiséis. Confirm `veintiún años`, `treinta y un años` and exact `cien`.
4. **Names and contact details.** `Muñoz` must retain ñ in the surname field; the supplied email uses `munoz`. Check that this distinction is clear rather than contradictory. Multiple surnames are acknowledged; examples do not define a universal naming system. Phone numbers are fictional teaching examples, not numbers to call. Email examples use IANA's reserved example.com domain.
5. **Audio quality.** There are 103 newly generated AI clips, using the existing voice system. Automated checks verify inventory, decodability and plausible speech ends. They do not verify pronunciation, omitted/repeated digits, voice appropriateness, natural pacing or timing between scene turns. Listen at normal and slow speed, then test native-device playback.
6. **Load and motivation.** Ask beginners what they are trying to accomplish at each stage and what comes next. Watch the number tables and alphabet screens especially. Changed situations and optional examples support personal use, but their learning benefit must be observed rather than assumed.

The first-party references checked for the new content include [RAE cardinal numbers](https://www.rae.es/dpd/cardinales), [RAE alphabet](https://www.rae.es/dpd/abecedario), [RAE v](https://www.rae.es/dpd/v), [RAE w](https://www.rae.es/dpd/w), [RAE y](https://www.rae.es/dpd/y), [RAE guion](https://www.rae.es/dpd/guion), [Cervantes specific notions A1–A2](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/niveles/09_nociones_especificas_inventario_a1-a2.htm) and [IANA example domains](https://www.iana.org/help/example-domains). ## Feedback and evidence boundaries

- New dictation steps hide the transcript, including accessible play labels, until the learner requests **Read instead** or completes the exercise. Slow playback remains available. Text help records the objective item as assisted, including after a reload; it does not block completion.
- The form preserves correct entries, marks incorrect fields individually, focuses the first field to fix and keeps drafts across leaving/reloading. A wrong phone number can be fixed without retyping the name or email. The entire form contributes one objective item, not five separate official marks.
- Exact detail grading keeps leading zeros, accents/ñ in names, and email punctuation. Phone grouping spaces, parentheses and hyphens are accepted. Names ignore case and normalise composed Unicode; email domain case is ignored while the local part stays exact. New explicit spelling exercises require the taught spelling. General phrase exercises retain the app's existing accent feedback policy.
- Speaking models and changed situations remain optional. Speaking is unrecorded self-practice. A learner tapping **I said it aloud** is not pronunciation, fluency or oral-exam evidence.
- The registration is a five-field precursor with English support and unlimited practice. It is not the complete DELE W1 task or a timed/mock exam. Full writing, interaction, cumulative checkpoints and calibrated readiness evidence remain planned.
